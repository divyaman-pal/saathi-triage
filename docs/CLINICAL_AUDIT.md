# SAATHI V2 — Clinical and Statistical Audit

> **Read this before believing anything else in this repository.**

Every number below comes from **simulated data generated by a process we wrote**
(`saathi/data/cohort.py`). A model fitted to that process recovers that process. **None of
this is evidence about clinical performance in any emergency department.**

The purpose of this document is to state the methodology precisely enough that the
limitations are visible, and to record the places where the system is weak.

Reproduce with `python -m saathi.eval.evaluate`. Full output: `artifacts/evaluation_report.txt`.

---

## 0. A reproducibility defect, found and fixed

The evaluation was **not reproducible across processes** until late in the build. Identical
seeds produced sensitivity varying by ~0.03 and AUC by ~0.02 between runs, and even the
*deterministic* arrival-rules layer moved.

Cause: `saathi/data/simulate.py` seeded simulated rPPG observation noise with Python's
builtin `hash()` on a string. `hash()` for `str` is randomised per process under
`PYTHONHASHSEED`, so the jitter differed every run — occasionally by enough to move a vital
across a threshold and change an assigned acuity.

Fixed by replacing it with a stable `blake2b` digest. Runs are now bit-identical across
processes and platforms.

It is recorded here rather than quietly patched because it is the kind of defect that makes
every other number in a submission meaningless, and because **a generative process that
cannot be reproduced cannot be audited**, which defeats the point of stating it.

## 1. Discrimination is not calibration

AUC is close to useless as a headline metric here. It summarises ranking across every
possible threshold, and we do not operate at every threshold — we operate at exactly one,
derived from a cost ratio.

```
AUC (model)      0.709        reported once, then set aside
AUC (rules-only) 0.542
```

**The headline numbers**, at the operating point `p ≥ 0.0769`:

| Metric | Value | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| **Sensitivity** | **0.391** | [0.285, 0.509] |
| **Under-triage rate** | **0.609** | [0.491, 0.715] |
| Specificity | 0.850 | [0.831, 0.867] |
| Over-triage rate | 0.150 | [0.133, 0.169] |
| Escalation rate | 0.161 | [0.143, 0.179] |

Sensitivity per ground-truth arrival acuity — the model is much better at the patients it
matters most for, which is the right direction:

| Truth level | Sensitivity | 95% CI | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | not reported — 2 events | | 2 |
| L2 | **0.652** | [0.449, 0.812] | 23 |
| L3 | 0.290 | [0.161, 0.466] | 31 |
| L4 | 0.167 | [0.047, 0.448] | 12 |
| L5 | not reported — 1 event | | 1 |

Groups with too few events are **not reported** rather than reported with a meaningless
point estimate.

### Calibration-in-the-large, per age group

Because a model with good discrimination and bad calibration in the elderly is a safety
hazard, not an achievement.

| Group | n | Mean predicted | Observed | Bias | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paediatric | 385 | 0.0390 | 0.0364 | **+0.0027** | over-predicts — safe |
| Adult | 884 | 0.0399 | 0.0486 | **−0.0087** | **under-predicts — unsafe** |
| Geriatric | 331 | 0.0504 | 0.0363 | **+0.0141** | over-predicts — safe |

**The adult bias is negative and this is the largest group in the cohort.** Under-prediction
is the unsafe direction. It is small in absolute terms (0.9 percentage points against a 4.9%
event rate) but it is systematic, and it is in the wrong direction for the group that
supplies most of the queue.

The geriatric bias is currently positive — the safe direction — but it has been **negative on
other training runs at smaller sample sizes.** A calibration that changes sign when you
retrain is not a calibration you should rely on. The correct response is a **subgroup
calibration gate before deployment**, not a better number in a table.

## 2. Prediction is not diagnosis

SAATHI predicts *risk of deterioration and need for escalation*. It does not diagnose.

This is enforced, not stated: diagnostic nouns are in the claim grammar's universally
forbidden lexicon, and the validator rejects them in **every** epistemic state. There is no
output label, UI string or model claim in this system that names a disease.

The distinction extends to the language of attribution:

| Type | Permitted | Forbidden |
|---|---|---|
| Attribution | "Respiratory rate contributed most to the score change" | — |
| Association | "This pattern was associated with escalation in similar presentations" | — |
| Causation | — | "Stillness caused the deterioration" |

A SHAP contribution licenses attribution language. It licenses nothing causal. **A feature
attribution is never presented as a clinical mechanism.**

## 3. The transfer problem

**MIMIC-IV-ED is a single US academic centre. NHAMCS is a US ambulatory survey. Neither
describes an Indian district emergency department.**

**No transfer claim is made.** Any pre-training would be **frozen and treated as a prior**,
never as a validated starting point.

The distribution shift is not marginal, and it spans:

| Dimension | Why it shifts |
|---|---|
| Case mix | Infectious disease burden, trauma mechanism, injury patterns |
| Arrival mode | Ambulance penetration is a fraction of US rates; most patients walk in or are carried |
| Staffing ratio | 4:1 attendant-to-patient; triage nurse load is not comparable |
| Documentation practice | Structured EHR fields largely absent |
| Baseline physiology | Anaemia prevalence, nutritional status, chronic disease burden |
| Presentation delay | Patients arrive later in the disease course |

### The validation protocol

1. **Rules-only cold start** at any new site. No learned parameters. Its performance is
   measured and published, because that is what the first month actually delivers
2. **Prospective collection in shadow mode**, 30 days, scoring silently
3. **Federated evaluation via BODH / ABDM**, per site and per subgroup — SAATHI can be
   benchmarked on Indian data **without ever holding it**
4. **Site-specific calibration** behind a human review gate
5. **Continuous subgroup monitoring**, with the pre-committed stopping conditions in
   [ADOPTION.md](ADOPTION.md#9-what-would-make-us-stop)

### What rules-only actually delivers — the number nobody publishes

| Metric | Value | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| Exact agreement with truth acuity | 0.456 | [0.431, 0.480] |
| Under-triage, any | 0.076 | [0.064, 0.090] |
| **Under-triage, L1/L2 missed to L3+** | **0.062** | [0.051, 0.075] |
| Over-triage, any | 0.469 | [0.444, 0.493] |

This is the honest first-month picture, and it is what a site gets on day one.

## 4. Age stratification — necessary, and not sufficient

The whole architecture exists to prevent silent age-related safety risk. It works partially,
and the evaluation says exactly where it does not.

**Under-triage by age band, arrival rules layer:**

| Age band | Under-triage | 95% CI | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| age_5_12 | **0.000** | [0.000, 0.024] | 157 |
| age_18_65 | 0.025 | [0.017, 0.037] | 884 |
| age_12_18 | 0.060 | [0.029, 0.119] | 116 |
| age_1_5 | 0.121 | [0.072, 0.197] | 107 |
| age_80_plus | 0.148 | [0.088, 0.236] | 88 |
| **age_65_80** | **0.181** | **[0.138, 0.234]** | **243** |

**The elderly bands are the worst performers by a factor of seven against working-age
adults, and the intervals do not overlap.** This is not noise.

It is also not surprising, and stating why matters more than the number. The elderly present
atypically: blunted febrile response, absent tachycardia, altered mental status as the only
sign, a lower physiological reserve that is invisible in a snapshot. Encoding *"absence of
fever does not lower risk"* into the geriatric thresholds catches some of that. It does not
catch a patient whose only abnormality is that they are not quite themselves — which is
precisely why the **attendant channel** exists, and why **materiality can escalate without
statistical support**.

**This is the single largest clinical weakness in the system**, and it is the exact failure
the architecture was built to address. It is improved, not solved.

## 5. Asymmetric cost — the persuasive artefact

```
C(under-triage) = 12.0     C(over-triage) = 1.0     ratio 12:1
threshold = 1 / (1 + 12) = 0.0769
```

| | Symmetric (0.5) | Cost-derived (0.0769) |
|---|---|---|
| Sensitivity | **0.000** | 0.391 |
| Under-triage rate | 1.000 | 0.609 |
| Over-triage rate | 0.001 | 0.150 |
| Patients under-triaged | 69 | 42 |
| Patients over-triaged | 1 | 230 |
| **Policy cost per 100 patients** | 51.81 | **45.88** |

**24 named patients are left in the waiting room by the accuracy-optimised threshold.**

The symmetric column reading 0.000 is not a broken comparison — it is the argument. With a
4.3% event rate, a *well-calibrated* model should almost never emit `p > 0.5`, because more
than half of such patients would genuinely have to deteriorate. An accuracy-optimised
threshold on a rare event is not conservative; it is **inert**.

Choosing 0.5 is not choosing "no policy". It is silently choosing a 1:1 cost ratio, and
asserting that an unattended deterioration and a wasted nurse walk are equally bad.

### Sensitivity to the ratio

Required by `cost_policy.yaml`, because a conclusion that holds only at exactly 12:1 is an
artefact of the parameter rather than a property of the system:

| Ratio | Threshold | Sensitivity | Escalation rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6:1 | 0.1429 | 0.116 | 0.028 |
| **12:1** | **0.0769** | **0.362** | **0.157** |
| 24:1 | 0.0400 | 0.565 | 0.293 |

The trade is visible and monotone: more sensitivity costs more nurse walks, linearly and
predictably. The choice of 12 is a **clinical governance decision**, which is why it lives in
a versioned policy file that a hospital can change and audit rather than in code.

## 6. What does the learned model actually add?

The question most submissions avoid.

| Policy | Sensitivity | Under-triage | Cost/100 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Model** at cost threshold | **0.391** | 0.609 | 45.88 |
| **Rules-only**, matched to the same escalation rate | 0.232 | 0.768 | 54.87 |
| Escalate everyone | 1.000 | 0.000 | 95.69 |

**The learned model is worth +0.159 sensitivity at identical nurse load.** Matching the
escalation rate is the only fair comparison — a model that simply escalates more is not
better, it is louder.

"Escalate everyone" never misses anyone and would be ignored by the second shift. It is in
the table to make the point that sensitivity alone is not a goal.

### The nurse-alone baseline, and a comparison that must be made carefully

```
Of the 69 patients who deteriorated during the wait, the arrival snapshot had
already placed 34 at L1/L2. The remaining 35 were correctly triaged on arrival
and then not looked at again.

nurse-alone sensitivity to in-wait deterioration:  0.493  [0.378, 0.608]
```

**This 0.493 is higher than the model's 0.391, and the two numbers are not comparable.**
Saying "the model is worse than the nurse" would be the wrong reading, and so would ignoring
the comparison.

They measure different things. The nurse-alone figure counts patients who were *already
prioritised at arrival* — the arrival decision was right, and those patients were going to be
seen. The model figure counts patients the model *flagged during the wait*. The populations
overlap and the mechanisms are different.

**The gap SAATHI exists to close is the 35 patients who were correctly triaged and then never
looked at again.** This is not a claim that the nurse was wrong. It is a claim that the
arrival decision was the only decision anybody made.

## 7. The whole system, not the model

The model is one of seven escalation paths. Running the real pipeline over a held-out cohort:

```
Held-out: 260 encounters, 9 deteriorated during the wait

urgency actually RAISED for a deteriorating patient    0.444  [0.189, 0.733]
ANY action taken (raised, flagged, re-check forced)    1.000  [0.701, 1.000]   <-- safety net

urgency raised for a NON-deteriorating patient         0.187  [0.144, 0.240]   <-- the price
any action for a NON-deteriorating patient             0.713  [0.654, 0.766]
```

Two honest readings are required here.

**First**, `1.000 [0.701, 1.000]` on n=9 is a wide interval on a small number. It is
encouraging and it is not a claim of reliability. Nine events cannot establish a safety net.

**Second**, `0.713` of non-deteriorating patients received *some* action. That sounds alarming
until you see what the actions are — mostly SLA-driven re-checks, which is the system doing
exactly what it is designed to do (every patient has a maximum safe wait, and breaching it
forces a look). The number that reflects alert load is `0.187`, urgency actually raised.
Presenting `0.713` without that distinction would be scaremongering; hiding it would be worse.

**Which paths caught the deteriorating patients whose urgency rose:**

```
ARRIVAL                            3
TRAJECTORY_MATERIAL_WORSENING      1
```

Read that carefully. **The model does not appear.** The patients whose urgency rose were
protected by the arrival rules and by trajectory materiality — by the *contract and the
rules*, not by the learned model. On this cohort, the deterministic layer is what is doing
the work.

That is a finding about the simulation as much as about the system, but it is the honest
reading of the table, and it is why the deterministic layer is the floor of the whole design
rather than a fallback.

## 8. Class imbalance and label leakage

**Event rate 0.043** — 69 of 1600. Overall accuracy is meaningless at this base rate; a model
predicting "no deterioration" for everyone scores 95.7%. Everything above is reported on the
rare class.

**Leakage audit.** Any feature recorded *because a clinician was already worried* is leakage.
Excluded from the feature set:

- A second set of vitals taken early (taken because someone was concerned)
- A monitor being attached
- Bay assignment
- Any nurse re-check triggered by the system's own escalation

**Label definition and its limits.** The label is *deterioration onset within 30 minutes*, and
nothing else. This is deliberate — a composite label collapses the arrival layer and the
deterioration layer and hides which one is working.

Four candidate targets carry four different biases, and they are **not interchangeable**:

| Target | Bias |
|---|---|
| ICU admission within 24 h | Bed availability, admission policy, insurance status |
| Critical intervention within 6 h | Who was available to intervene |
| Mortality | Rare; dominated by pre-hospital factors |
| ESI-level agreement | Agreement with a human who may be wrong; encodes existing under-triage |

## 9. rPPG validity limits — stated, not overclaimed

- **Heart rate from video** has meaningful published support under controlled conditions
- **Respiratory rate from video, in a crowded and poorly lit waiting room, does not.** It is
  treated as a low-weight, high-uncertainty signal: reliability weight **0.35**, with an SNR
  floor that rejects it outright rather than down-weighting it
- **Skin-tone bias is real.** SNR degrades at higher Fitzpatrick types under standard
  pipelines. Fitzpatrick-stratified recruitment is mandatory in any prospective validation,
  and subgroup SNR is a **reported, monitored metric**
- **Motion, occlusion and distance are the dominant error sources — and they all correlate
  with crowding, which correlates with when the system matters most.** Missingness here is
  emphatically **not at random**, and the architecture models the correlation: under surge,
  camera confidence falls, abstention rises, and re-check intervals shorten
- The **quality floor** rejects values outright rather than down-weighting them, because a
  down-weighted value we do not believe can still move a score

## 10. Fairness — including the uncomfortable rows

Reported with confidence intervals, because point estimates on 8 events are not measurements.

**By Fitzpatrick skin-tone band:**

| Band | Sensitivity | 95% CI | events |
|---|---|---|---|
| I–II | 0.250 | [0.071, 0.591] | 8 |
| III–IV | 0.391 | [0.264, 0.535] | 46 |
| V–VI | 0.467 | [0.248, 0.699] | 15 |

**These intervals overlap across almost their entire range, and the ordering is the opposite
of the injected penalty's direction.** With 8 events in the lightest band, this subgroup has
not been measured. Reporting the point estimates without saying so would be dishonest in
either direction — and the temptation to quote "V–VI performs best" as a fairness result is
exactly the trap this table exists to avoid.

The skin-tone gap in the simulator is **injected by us** to make the monitoring path visible.
It demonstrates that the metric is watched. **It is not a measurement of real-world rPPG
bias.**

**By sex:**

| Sex | Sensitivity | 95% CI | events |
|---|---|---|---|
| F | 0.282 | [0.165, 0.438] | 39 |
| M | 0.533 | [0.361, 0.698] | 30 |

A 0.25 gap with barely-overlapping intervals and **no mechanism we can point to in the
generative process.** We do not know why. It could be an artefact of how latent severity and
sex interact in the simulator; it could be a genuine asymmetry we introduced without
intending to. **An unexplained subgroup gap is a finding, not a footnote**, and it would be a
blocking issue before any deployment.

**By attendant present:**

| | Sensitivity | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| Present | 0.380 | [0.259, 0.518] |
| Absent | 0.421 | [0.231, 0.637] |

Substantially equivalent, which is the desired result. If performance depended on whether a
family member came along, that would be an equity problem — an unaccompanied patient is often
the more vulnerable one — and it belongs on the front page of the metrics view, which is
where it is.

**By prior record:**

| | Sensitivity | 95% CI | Escalation rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Linked | 0.276 | [0.147, 0.457] | 0.116 |
| None | 0.475 | [0.329, 0.625] | 0.199 |

Counter-intuitive at first reading, and explained by the escalation rate column: the
zero-history path escalates more often, because reduced information shortens re-check
intervals and lowers confidence, which raises rank. **The observation-only route is more
cautious, by design.** Higher sensitivity here is the compensation mechanism working, not
evidence that history is useless.

## 11. Human–AI interaction effects

**None of this is measured. It cannot be, with simulated data.**

**Automation bias.** A displayed recommendation changes a nurse's judgement. A model that
improves paper accuracy while anchoring nurses to wrong answers is **net harmful**, and
nothing in this repository would detect that. The 30-day shadow-mode arm exists specifically
to produce an uncontaminated baseline of nurse decisions to compare against.

**Alarm fatigue.** Escalation rate has a hard budget — 8/hour normal, 12/hour surge. At the
measured 0.161 escalation rate and Tier A volume, that is ~3.4 escalations/hour, within
budget. **If SAATHI escalates more than the floor can absorb, it will be ignored within a
week**, and the physician view states the shortfall as a number rather than absorbing it
silently.

**Feedback loop — real and unhandled.** If SAATHI escalates a patient who is then seen sooner
and does well, **the label is contaminated**: the outcome was changed by the prediction. Any
retraining must account for this and none of the analysis above does. Handling it properly
requires either a hold-out arm that does not receive the intervention — which has an obvious
ethical cost — or instrumental-variable methods on the escalation threshold. We flag it as
unsolved rather than claiming a solution.

## 13. What would change our minds

- If prospective data showed the elderly under-triage gap **widening** rather than narrowing,
  the age-band approach is insufficient and the geriatric path needs a different design, not
  a tuned threshold
- If nurse agreement rose over time **without outcome improvement**, that is anchoring, and
  the display should be pulled
- If the deterministic layer alone matched the full system on real data, **the models should
  be removed** — they would be adding cost, latency and opacity for nothing
- If override rate stayed above 40%, the floor does not believe it, and an ignored
  recommendation carries anchoring risk with no upside
